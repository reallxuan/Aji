Candidates and marks on the black boards

The stones are the owner's choice, K2 black and W3 white. Each cell has the four candidate tiers (best, close, doubtful, poor, from upper left to lower right). The close one is the move actually played in the record, so it has the outer ring. There is also a variation badge "B", a letter "a" on an empty point, and the position estimate's squares at the lower right.

How are the candidate discs drawn?

D1 UnchangedToday's paper discs and cinnabar rings, the same on every board (as P2 M1 decided)
D2 Dimmed paperStill paper, brought down to about W3's brightness, so no longer brighter than the white stones
D3 Dark discsDark grey with light text; brighter cinnabar rings for best and close, grey-brown for doubtful and poor
A 石墨 (Graphite)#2A2A2E
B 墨黑 (Ink)#0E0E10

Changes the same in all three columns (proposed)

  • The ring of the move actually played turns from dark brown to light, or it cannot be seen on black.
  • The variation badge turns light with a dark letter; the amber try-out badge is unchanged.
  • Marks and letters on empty points are light, as on today's walnut board.
  • The position estimate's squares keep today's colours. The black square already has a light rim, which is what shows it on black.

With D2 or D3, the rank chips in the candidates table, drawn after the discs, would have to change with the board too, or the table and the board would not match.
